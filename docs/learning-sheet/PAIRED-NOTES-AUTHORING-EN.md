## 3. Classroom Note for teaching

1. Start with the sheet title and a line for the student's name and class. Add screen links to the lab and the matching handout.
2. Review the terms and equations students need for the task. The example uses neutralisation, pH and pOH, titration, the endpoint, the equivalence point and the phosphate buffer pair.
3. Show the equipment diagram or graph and give students a table for their own readings. Include headings, substance names, units and the order of readings. Label any sample data clearly.
4. Ask one short check-out question based on an observation. For example, “After you add the same amount of acid, which solution changes pH more? Which readings show this?” Keep longer calculations for the worked examples or Exit Ticket.
5. Order examples by the method students need to learn. Give each example a useful title. The chemistry example moves from 1:1 to 1:2 reaction ratios and from a rising graph to a falling graph or a graph with two curves.
6. Give each subquestion its own writing space. Add a short starting hint when it helps. If students need a derivation first, finish the derivation and explain what the experiment shows before the practice.
7. Keep lines, table headings, substance names and units visible in the student edition. Show reasoning and worked steps in the answer edition. Keep space for each group's own lab readings.

## 4. Exit Ticket passages and practice

### Gap-fill passages from the lab

Write each passage as a short account of what the students did, what the results mean and what they can do next. Use common mix-ups as extra choices, such as endpoint versus equivalence point or volume versus amount in moles.

- In the example, Passage A uses the burette and graph. Passage B uses pH and buffers. Each passage has six gaps, a Word Bank of ten terms and a Sentence Bank of four choices. Passage A uses five terms and one sentence. Passage B uses four terms and two sentences.
- Label word choices `W01` and up, sentence choices `S01` and up, and gaps `A1`–`A6` or `B1`–`B6`. Use the same IDs in both languages. Mix the order of the choices.
- Use two columns inside the Word Bank. Put the two banks side by side on large screens and paper. Stack them on small screens. Give a full-width line for a sentence answer.
- Check that one choice fits each gap best in both languages. When answers are shown, display the complete passage and make the filled answers easy to spot.

For example, “The students recorded the first stable colour at the `A1`. They used the burette reading to estimate the volume needed for the acid and base to react in the correct ratio at the `A2`.” The Word Bank can include endpoint, equivalence point, half-equivalence point and other choices from the same topic.

### New questions and chapter-review practice

- Give the situation first, then the known values and units, then the task. Name the quantity in the question before using its symbol in the working.
- Group subquestions that use the same situation. Start by reading or processing data, then ask students to choose a method or explain the result. Say when they need an earlier answer.
- Put writing space next to each subquestion. Use clear task verbs such as identify, calculate, write the ionic equation, or choose and explain from the graph. Leave space for tables and multi-step calculations.
- In the worked reference, questions 1–7 cover burette readings and excess reagent, the salt at equivalence, acidic and basic buffers, buffer limits, a salt from a weak acid and weak base, and graphs and indicators. Change the substances or numbers when students need to apply the same skill to a new case.
- Label original questions based on chapter-review skills as **IPST chapter-review-style practice**. Record the chapter and question numbers used as references. The chemistry set uses chapter 10, questions 5–14. Questions 1–4 of the book review acid–base theory and ionisation. [IPST Grade 11 Chemistry Teacher Guide, Volume 4](https://www.scimath.org/ebook-chemistry/item/10299-4)
- Separate **Check Answer** from the full solution. The student printout can show an end result for self-checking when intended. The example uses `printResult:false` for A-Level items so their numerical answers stay hidden on the student printout.

## 5. Revision Notes for students

Use the completed Classroom Note from the same source. Keep the headings, definitions, examples, tables, equation numbers and answers in step with the standalone sheet. You can change the layout to suit the Exit Ticket. The example uses `classroom(kit, true)` in `data-print-zone="learning"` and `classroom(kit, false)` for the standalone Classroom Note.

Show the worked examples in Revision Notes in both editions. Keep the answers to practice and Recall under their own controls. Keep a place for each group's own readings and label any modelled or sample data with its source and conditions.

Try one question from each main skill with the student edition open. A student should be able to find the needed method, graph or data on the sheet.

## 6. Knowledge Recall

Ask short questions that students can answer from memory. For example, ask what salt hydrolysis means, which ion accepts a proton, or where to mark the equivalence point on a graph. A definition, equation, short explanation or marked point is usually enough.

- Move from definitions and parts of a system to choosing a method and then reading a graph.
- Number Recall questions separately. Keep a shared graph close to the questions that use it. Include axes and units in the student edition. Add answer labels only in the answer edition.
- The chemistry example covers titration, endpoint, equivalence point, buffers, conjugate pairs, excess HCl, hydrolysis, half-equivalence, pH and pKₐ, and indicator ranges.
- Match the answer space to the task. Keep all Recall answers hidden in the student printout.

## 7. Language, calculations, figures and sources

| Item | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Original questions and examples | Use full sentences in language secondary students can follow. Say what happened, give quantities and units, and state the task. |
| Thai and English | Put the Thai block first in HTML and provide the complete English block. Match question numbers, values, units, choices, graphs, conditions and answers. |
| Equations | Use LaTeX with Hub MathJax. Box key equations near the point of use. Number them separately from questions and state conditions such as 25 °C. |
| Worked answers | Say what method you will use. Write the equation, substitute values, cancel units and give the final result with units. Put one step on each line. Use `\frac{...}{...}` and `aligned` for longer working. |
| Chemistry calculations | Start with the balanced equation and the substance left over. Use an ICF (initial–change–final) table for a reaction that goes almost to completion. Keep the units the same throughout the table. Divide mmol by the total volume in mL to get mol/L before the ICE (initial–change–equilibrium) table when equilibrium matters. Check buffer limits and approximations. |
| Graphs and tables | Label axes, units and the range of values. Use solid and dashed lines as well as colour. Keep labels clear of curves and inside the figure in both languages and grayscale. |
| Sources | Give the document or creator, page or section, and a direct link. Identify lab photos, model graphs and exam graphs in captions. See the [lab image source notes](../../public/content/chemistry/ipst/acid-base/assets/lab-guide/SOURCES.md). |

In a titration calculation, subtract the initial burette reading from the final reading. Multiply delivered volume in mL by concentration in mmol/mL to get mmol of HCl, then use the balanced equation. When working out pH from excess reagent, name the volume you divide by and explain why it is the right volume.

## 8. Screen layout and A4 printing

| Item | Reference layout |
| --- | --- |
| Screen header | Put subject, topic number and title on the left. Put the sheet type and status on the right. Follow with the document title, name and class line, and section links. |
| Print header | Use the subject, topic number and title on the left. Put sheet type and status on the right. A thin line separates the header from the work. |
| Print footer | Use **Nix-century** on the left and **Page x / y** on the right, with a thin line above. Start at page 1 for each selected print range. |
| Name and class | Place them on the same line below the title, at 8 pt. Give more room for the name and show the line on the first page of a set with an opening header. |
| New sheets | Use 10 pt for body text, questions and working, and 11 pt for headings. Some major headings in the published example print at 13 pt. |
| Desktop and Android | Use A4 portrait, 210 × 297 mm, with `@page margin:0` and the paired worksheet spacing. It uses 15 mm at the sides, 11 mm at the top and bottom, and 3 mm between header, body and footer. Choose actual size (100%) and switch off browser headers and footers. |
| iPad and iPhone | Use the device-margin option for A4 portrait with 15 mm margins and 180 mm of content width. Recheck the preview because the pages break again at the same text size. |
| Writing space | Use `--sheet-print-height` to allow room for the task. The example uses about 14 mm for short Recall items and 16–26 mm for several calculation items. Add more for longer working, tables or graphs. |
| Page breaks | Keep the heading with the first item and keep a question with its figure or table. Use `data-print-keep` for a block that fits on one page or `data-print-keep-blank` when only its blank edition fits. |
| Small screens | At 560 px or below, stack figures and choice banks. Check equations, graphs and tables in portrait and landscape. |

For Exit Ticket printing, choose the whole set or one of three ranges. Use **Passages and practice**, **completed Classroom Note**, or **Knowledge Recall**. In the whole set, Notes and Recall start on new pages.

| Range | Student edition | Answer edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standalone Classroom Note | Writing lines, workspace and tables | Explanations, formulas and worked examples |
| Passages and practice | Gaps, workspace and any chosen Check Answers | Completed passages and worked answers |
| Revision Notes inside the Exit Ticket | Completed notes and worked examples | Completed notes and worked examples |
| Knowledge Recall | Questions, workspace and any question graph | Answers and labelled graphs when needed |
| Full Exit Ticket | All three ranges in the student edition | All three ranges in the answer edition |
