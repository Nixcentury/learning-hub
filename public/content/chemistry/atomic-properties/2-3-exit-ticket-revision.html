Atomic electrons occupy (1)principal energy levels, identified by the principal quantum number n = 1, 2, 3, … . The theoretical maximum electron capacity of shell n is given by (2)2n²; thus n = 3 can hold up to 18 electrons.

Each shell is divided into (3)subshells such as s, p, d and f, as allowed by n. The regions describing where electrons are likely to be found are called (4)orbitals. Each such region can accommodate at most (5)two electrons, provided that their spins are opposite.

In the ground state, electrons occupy lower-energy orbitals first under the (6)Aufbau principle. The (7)Pauli exclusion principle requires two electrons sharing one orbital to have opposite spins.

Question 11 | Phosphorus, Z = 153 marks

A neutral phosphorus atom (P) has atomic number Z = 15. Write its ground-state electron configuration and analyse its shells.

(a) Write the complete subshell configuration (1 mark).
(b) Write electron counts by shell, from low n to high n (1 mark).
(c) State the highest occupied n and the valence-electron count (1 mark).
Solution and reasoning
(a) e = Z = 15. Fill 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p³ (2+2+6+2+3 = 15 electrons).
(b) Group by n: n=1 has 2 e, n=2 has 8 e, n=3 has 5 e ⇒ 2, 8, 5.
(c) Highest occupied shell n = 3; valence electrons = 3s² + 3p³ = 5.
Question 12 | O orbital-box diagram3 marks

A neutral oxygen atom has Z = 8 and configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁴. Use ↑ and ↓ to show electron spin in the three equal-energy 2p orbitals.

(a) Draw the 2p⁴ orbital-box arrows (1 mark).
(b) State the number of unpaired electrons (1 mark).
(c) Explain using Hund’s rule and the Pauli principle (1 mark).
Solution and reasoning
(a) Place one ↑ in each 2p orbital, then pair ↓ in one: [↑↓] [↑] [↑] (any equivalent box order is valid).
(b) Two boxes have a single electron; there are 2 unpaired electrons.
(c) Hund: singly occupy degenerate orbitals with parallel spins first. Pauli: electrons paired within one orbital must have opposite spins.
Question 13 | Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ (A-Level extension)4 marks

Iron (Fe) has atomic number Z = 26. Write noble-gas shorthand configurations for the neutral atom and two cations, and identify the first subshell from which electrons are removed.

(a) Neutral Fe shorthand (1 mark).
(b) Fe²⁺ shorthand (1 mark).
(c) Fe³⁺ shorthand (1 mark).
(d) Explain why 4s electrons leave before 3d (1 mark).
Solution and reasoning
(a) [Ar] contributes 18 e; add 4s² 3d⁶ for the remaining 8 ⇒ Fe: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶.
(b) Fe²⁺ has e = 26 − 2 = 24; remove 4s² ⇒ [Ar] 3d⁶.
(c) Fe³⁺ has e = 26 − 3 = 23; remove 4s² and then one 3d ⇒ [Ar] 3d⁵.
(d) In Fe, 4s electrons are less tightly bound than 3d electrons and are easier to remove. Thus 4s (n=4) electrons leave before 3d (n=3); ionisation is not simply the reverse of the filling sequence.

2.3 Electron Configurations | Essential Principles

CHEMISTRY · M.4 · Pre-quiz review · 1/2

In a ground-state atom, electrons occupy principal shells (n) and subshells s, p, d and f according to energy. Orbitals describe the probable location of electrons, not fixed planetary orbits. Capacity and filling rules are both needed to write configurations.

Principal shells and capacity

The main shells are n = 1, 2, 3, … (K, L, M, …). Their theoretical maximum capacities follow 2n²: for n=1, 2, 3, 4 the maxima are 2, 8, 18 and 32. These are capacities, not necessarily actual occupancies.

One orbital holds at most 2 opposite-spin electrons. Subshells s, p, d, f comprise 1, 3, 5 and 7 orbitals respectively. Allowed subshells depend on n: n=1 has s; n=2 has s,p; n=3 has s,p,d; n=4 has s,p,d,f.

RuleHow it applies

Key filling order

1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d → 5p → 6s

2.3 Filling Rules | Ions and A-Level Connections

CHEMISTRY · M.4 · Pre-quiz review · 2/2

Noble-gas shorthand replaces completed inner shells with the preceding noble gas symbol: Na: [Ne] 3s¹; Cl: [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵. In transition metals, filling order and ionisation order are not identical.

Shorthand examples and exceptions

SpeciesShorthandObservation
Ca (20)[Ar] 4s²2,8,8,2
Fe (26)[Ar] 4s² 3d⁶Remove 4s first for cations
Fe²⁺[Ar] 3d⁶4s² removed
Fe³⁺[Ar] 3d⁵4s² + one 3d removed
Cr (24)*[Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵Exception: half-filled 3d
Cu (29)*[Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰Exception: filled 3d

Which electrons are removed first?

For a transition-metal cation, remove electrons from the highest occupied principal shell first: Fe loses 4s before 3d, not simply the reverse of its neutral-atom filling order.

A-Level-style worked reasoning (not a copied exam item)

A-Level-style worked extension: neutral Hf (Z=72) has [Xe] 4f¹⁴ 5d² 6s². In HfO₂, Hf has oxidation state +4. For formal ionic electron counting, Hf⁴⁺ is [Xe] 4f¹⁴ after losing 6s² and 5d². At n=5, 5s² + 5p⁶ remain, giving 2+6 = 8 electrons. Note that 4f belongs to n=4, not n=5.

A-Level reasoning checklist

  1. Check total electrons: neutral e = Z; ionic e = Z − signed charge.
  2. The number before each subshell letter determines n: 3d has n=3 even if it fills after 4s.
  3. Transition-metal cations usually lose ns before (n−1)d electrons.
  4. Cr and Cu differ from the simplest filling prediction. Half/full 3d stability helps rationalise them, but does not predict every exception.
  5. For transition metals, the meaning of valence electrons depends on context; do not apply the main-group rule blindly.
