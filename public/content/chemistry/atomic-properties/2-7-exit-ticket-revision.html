3. Balance nuclear equations and identify the daughter

The upper number A counts nucleons. For a nucleus, Z is the proton number. For an emitted particle, the lower number gives its charge in units of e, such as −1 for an electron. The symbol ∑ means the sum over all terms.

∑ Aleft = ∑ Aright(2)
∑ Zleft = ∑ Zright(3)
EmissionDaughter numbersReason
αA − 4, Z − 2Loses two protons and two neutrons
β⁻A, Z + 1One neutron changes into a proton
β⁺A, Z − 1One proton changes into a neutron
γA, ZThe nucleus drops to a lower energy state

The abbreviated beta equations are sufficient for this bookkeeping. The neutrino or antineutrino in the full equation has A = 0 and zero charge, so it does not alter either sum.

24 = 24 + 0

∑ Zleft = ∑ Zright

11 = 12 + (−1)

2411Na → 2412Mg + 0−1e

The emission is beta-minus.

4. Follow the halving pattern before using a formula

Point to 1, 2 and 3 half-lives. What fractions remain? What fraction has decayed after 3 half-lives?

The fractions remaining are 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8. After 3 half-lives the decayed fraction is 1 − 1/8 = 7/8.

Half-life is the time for the number of undecayed nuclei of the original isotope to halve. Individual decays are random. Half-life describes the behaviour of a large number of nuclei.

Let t be elapsed time, T½ be the half-life in the same units, and n be the number of half-lives.

n = t/T½(4)

The original isotope’s mass is multiplied by ½ at each interval. Let m0 be its initial mass, mt its undecayed mass and f the fraction remaining. This gives

f = mt/m0 = (½)n(5)

Let mdecay denote the mass of the original isotope that has decayed.

mdecay = m0 − mt(6)
