# Chemistry 10.1 diagrams

Original SVG diagrams authored for the Learning Hub guided notes. No textbook images are copied. Curved arrows start at the donor lone pair and end at the acceptor atom. Ammonia/boron trifluoride and phosphine/aluminium chloride products use adduct bond notation; their formal charges are not drawn. Hydroxide/carbon dioxide shows hydrogen carbonate with the overall negative charge.

Content reference: IPST Chemistry Book 4, Section 10.1, printed pp. 4–9, and Teacher’s Guide pp. 10–14.

The 50-ion list is a selected foundation and common-exam reference, not a claim to list every possible ion. The 17 everyday examples identify substances or uses; branded products are mixtures and may use different ingredients. Carbonates, hydrogen carbonates and hypochlorite are explicitly identified as salts with basic behaviour. Calcium carbonate is identified by its low solubility and reaction with acids rather than assigned a fixed solution pH. No numerical household-product pH values or preparation instructions are supplied.

## Adapted worksheet revision — 1 November 2026

The worksheet now uses `ammonia-boron-chloride.svg`, `ammonia-aluminium.svg`, and `chloride-boron.svg`. These original connectivity schematics match NH₃/BCl₃, NH₃/AlCl₃, and Cl⁻/BCl₃ in the revised examples/questions. Only the reacting lone pair is shown. Adduct notation is used for the neutral donor/acceptor products; BCl₄⁻ has brackets and the overall negative charge. These are connectivity diagrams, not molecular-geometry drawings.

The former SVGs remain available for cached copies. The removed worksheet items, textbook/teacher-guide crops and frozen SVGs are supplied to the teacher separately as a private preparation packet. The public [preparation notes](../../../../../../../docs/quiz/preparation/chemistry-acid-base-10-1/README.md) contain only the source-page and replacement mapping, with no scanned source images or private links. No new Quiz is published by this worksheet revision.
