## Scientific conventions and corrections

- All aqueous concentrations in calculations are in mol/L. Solution volume is converted to L for n = CV. Ion stoichiometry is applied before converting amount to mass. Stated molar masses are supplied for the mass problems.
- Conductivity diagram preserves equal 0.01 M concentrations, matching apparatus and temperature. Only HCl versus acetic acid, and NaOH versus ammonia, are compared. Brightness is qualitative; ion mobility also affects conductivity, so it is not presented as a numerical ion counter or a universal cross-substance strength test.
- The strong-acid list follows Table 10.1. H₂SO₄ is discussed separately: essentially complete first step, partial second step. Dissociation of the dissolved part of strong hydroxides is distinguished from solubility. Source idealized concentrations are retained; preparation problems assume the stated solution is attainable.
- C is the initial analytical concentration; x is the concentration ionized. The simple x²/(C−x) form is explicitly limited to the 1:1 equilibrium with no initially supplied product ions and negligible water contribution. It is not applied to the second sulfuric-acid step.
- The book's C/K > 1000 criterion is used consistently for the specified simple equilibrium. Only x in C−x is neglected. Failure of this adopted criterion leads to solving the full quadratic in the worksheet; it is not claimed as a universal mathematical prohibition of all approximations.
- Activity 10.1 is a calculation/comparison activity, not a wet-lab experiment. The graph uses unrounded square-root approximations at the four original concentrations. Percentages rounded in the table and plotted unrounded values are compatible.
- Original quadratic question C = 1.00×10⁻³, Ka = 1.00×10⁻⁵: the independently calculated positive root is 9.512492197×10⁻⁵ mol/L, reported as 9.51×10⁻⁵. The guide's 9.50×10⁻⁵ results from early rounding of its discriminant.
- Original Exercise Q4 retains Kb = 1.0×10⁻⁴, as in the student question; a guide substitution line printing 10×10⁻⁴ is not propagated.
- Original Exercise Q6 retains 0.25 and 0.40 mol/L throughout. The guide's last sentence printing 0.45 mol/L is not propagated.
- Original Exercise Q7 uses x = 0.00336 without premature rounding: Ka = 1.4171520386×10⁻⁵, reported as 1.4×10⁻⁵. The guide rounds x first and obtains 1.5×10⁻⁵. This difference is documented here without adding distracting editorial text to the student flow.
- Kw = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ is always tied to 25 °C. Neutrality means equal hydronium and hydroxide concentrations, including when temperature changes. KaKb = Kw is explicitly restricted to the same conjugate pair at the same temperature.
- Some reference-table rows have been selected for the actual problems; their constants are given data, not a demand to memorize every number. All question data needed for the original problems is supplied.
