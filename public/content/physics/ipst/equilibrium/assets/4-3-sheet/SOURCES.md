## Prepared vector diagrams

All 15 SVGs were drawn for this sheet. Colors consistently distinguish force vectors; purple `v` is velocity, gray dashed vectors are components. Captions identify illustrative experiment results, not collected measurements.

- `rest-and-motion.svg`, `normal-force-press.svg`: linked introductory comparisons; equal magnitudes use equal arrow lengths within the comparison.
- `smooth-wall-floor.svg`: smooth wall/floor force directions for understanding Q3; no torque calculation added.
- `three-force-result.svg`: vectors (3,0), (0,4), (−3,−4), arbitrary common scale; closed triangle.
- `four-force-result.svg`: (4,0), (0,3), (−2,−1), (−2,−2); closed quadrilateral, unequal forces.
- `force-components.svg`: equal 10 N forces with 30° measured from horizontal versus vertical; angles and components differ accordingly.
- `trig-triangle.svg`, `cosine-height.svg`: exact side/opposite-angle naming for derivations using altitude and Pythagoras.
- `bridge-force-triangle.svg`: exactly the first physics→triangle task. F₁=30 N right, F₂=20 N at 120° from +x. Internal included angle is supplementary (60°); F₃=10√7 N.
- `bridge-ropes-triangle.svg`: exactly the second bridge task. W=60 N; rope angles 30°/45° from horizontal; internal angles A=45°, B=60°, C=75°. Side names u/v/w avoid confusion with panels A/B and vertex labels.
- `symmetric-ropes.svg`, `ropes-vertical-angles.svg`: angles explicitly distinguish horizontal/vertical references. Symmetric rope arrows are schematic, not a cross-panel force scale.
- `incline-force-components.svg`: components are not additional forces; θ is between W and the inward normal component.
- `incline-up-down.svg`: F points upslope in both cases; friction opposes sliding. Collinear F/f arrows in the downward-motion panel are shifted along their line for legibility.
- `horizontal-pulley.svg`: staged two-body friction-limit preparation.

## Adaptations and independent checks

- Wording is shortened, givens restated and multi-part questions split into separate answer areas. Worked examples use W or T equations before arithmetic. Additional questions bridge observation, components, rope geometry, symmetric loading, static-vs-maximum friction and separate-body analysis.
- Exercise 4.3 Q3: teacher guide uses a scaled drawing (~26.5 N, ~100.5° from the left-pointing arrow). This sheet uses components: √700 N, 79.1066° above +x (=100.8934° from the left arrow). The graphical guide angle is approximate.
- Exercise 4.3 Q5: geometry is vertical 80 cm, horizontal 60 cm; F/40=60/80, so F=30 N, T=50 N. Do not copy the guide's transposed ratio.
- Example 4.2: vertical component of T₂ is T₂ sin θ₂, matching the diagram and final equations; corrects a prose slip in the student book.
- Chapter problem 3 retains student dimensions 0.60 m and 0.30 m (the guide uses 6 m and 3 m; ratio is identical).
- Chapter problem 7 is clarified as impending upward sliding, using limiting STATIC friction. This resolves the source phrase “moves upward just sufficiently.”
- g=9.8 m/s² when mass is supplied (the 5 kg traffic light weighs 49 N); given values labelled N are weights, not masses.
- Chapter problem 8 is accelerating (a=g/8), and problems 9–20 and most challenges concern torque or stability. They are not included as equilibrium practice here. Included challenges: 21, 25 and 26.
- The resultant-zero criterion concerns translational equilibrium. The sheet never imposes “every force passes through the center of mass.” Concurrency and common-line statements are explicitly scoped to rigid bodies remaining at rest without rotation.
- Independent trigonometric/component calculations and the matching guide were checked; browser/build verification is recorded in the root QA record.
