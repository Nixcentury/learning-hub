# Sources and current use — Physics 10.2

## Current worksheet revision: 2026-11-02-1

The live-sheet replacement in this PR uses independently authored problems and
vector diagrams. None of the 15 source JPEG crops is referenced by the revised
worksheet. All 15 crops remain byte-identical for future review quizzes. Their
hashes, old bilingual question fragments and full source context are preserved in
`docs/quiz/preparation/physics-double-slit-10-2/`.

The ten new vectors, their controlled data/geometry and the distinction between
models and measurements are documented in `replacements/SOURCES.md`. The existing
original `wavefronts.svg` remains in use; the older `slit-types.svg` is retained
but is not needed by this revision. No external photograph is embedded or used
as quantitative data. The optional Commons candidate is still only a reference
in the preparation proposal.

The source-derived previous examples, understanding checks, Exercise 10.2 and
previous Q21–25 are archived, not discarded. The revised sheet uses different
values, some different unknowns, interval counts and two-sided distances; it
contains four worked examples, 37 ordinary question groups, 50 individual
question rows and 64 separate note/answer slots per language. The 14 headed
knowledge notes and phase derivation remain, with point names matched to U,V,O,C,P.
The guide is authored for this exact revision and references actual slots/anchors.

The book and teacher guide disagree on chapter-end problem 8's spacing:
10⁻⁷ m in the student scan versus 10⁻³ m in the guide. This is recorded for the
future original-question Quiz; the new worksheet does not silently combine them.

All JPEGs are native PDF-region renderings. Student crops use 2.5× PDF coordinates and JPEG quality 94; the additional teacher-guide diagram uses 3× rendering. No ruler or path length is digitally stretched independently of its image.

`slit-types.svg` and `wavefronts.svg` are original explanatory diagrams made for this worksheet. They are qualitative and explicitly not to scale. The former is a front view showing parallel vertical openings: a is one opening's width, d is centre-to-centre separation. The latter is a top-view wavefront cross-section, with common incident plane wavefronts and spreading waves beyond each opening. They do not represent quantitative intensity plots.

Previous Questions 21–25 used the same numerical data as the guide's chapter-end problems 1–5 (teacher printed pp.152–154). Questions 26–30 are newly authored parallel practice. No third-party internet image is copied. The guide is also used to verify the original numerical answers, including the approximate 1.20–1.85 cm reading in Exercise 10.2 Q3.

All fringe formulas assume initially in-phase, monochromatic coherent sources in the same medium. Far-field geometry and small-angle screen formulas are distinguished. Complete minima additionally assume equal amplitudes. The finite-slit diffraction envelope is mentioned qualitatively; section 10.3 is outside this sheet's scope.
